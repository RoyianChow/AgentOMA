## What I completed

### 1. Built a prescription-intake demonstration for pharmacists

I added a new pharmacist-facing demonstration that takes one of several
completely invented prescription examples and organizes the written information
into a structured review draft.

The demonstration can highlight things a pharmacist should check, including:

- missing information, such as a quantity or signature;
- conflicting dates or quantities;
- unclear or noisy characters;
- mismatched prescriber details; and
- prescriptions that always require closer human review.

The system does not decide whether a prescription is valid and does not accuse
anyone of fraud or alteration. It describes what it observed and leaves every
decision to a pharmacist.

### 2. Kept the demonstration safely separated from real pharmacy work

I added several limits so the demonstration could not quietly become a real
prescription-processing tool:

- It accepts only a fixed set of invented examples. There is no upload box and
  no free-text prescription entry.
- It does not use patient information or real prescriptions.
- It does not save anything to the patient record or database.
- It does not send information to an AI model, outside company, or network
  service. The extraction is a predictable rules-based process.
- It cannot create an assessment, prescription record, claim, billing code, or
  payment outcome.
- Every result is marked for human review; there is no automatic acceptance.
- The feature is off by default and has both an expiry control and a global kill
  switch.
- The pharmacist page and its server action each check the safety gate
  independently.

This gave the team something concrete to evaluate without claiming that it is
ready for real documents. The detailed boundary is recorded in
[`AI-RX-06-synthetic-prescription-extraction.md`](../task-10/AI-RX-06-synthetic-prescription-extraction.md).

### 3. Added testing and a clear evaluation scorecard

I created automated checks for the parser, safety gate, synthetic-data boundary,
document-integrity warnings, user interface, and evaluation calculations.

The scorecard measures whether the fixed examples continue to produce the
expected output. It also checks that the demonstration does not invent missing
information and that every result still requires pharmacist review.

The recorded test score was intentionally described as a consistency check, not
proof that the tool works on unfamiliar real-world prescriptions. A separate
independent test set and pharmacist evaluation are still required before anyone
could consider broader use.

### 5. Advanced the patient messaging and reminder design

I completed and organized Task 07 planning through Workstream I. This work
describes how future reminders and secure portal messages should behave before
any live messaging system is built.

In plain language, the design established that:

- patients must give specific permission for the exact contact method and
  purpose;
- contact details and permission must be checked again immediately before a
  message is sent;
- opting out or suppressing a destination overrides schedules and retries;
- email, text messages, and push notifications must remain generic and must not
  reveal medical details;
- sensitive conversations belong only inside an authenticated secure portal;
- a failed message must never silently switch to another contact method;
- automated systems and AI must not decide whether a patient reply is urgent;
- delivery of a reminder does not prove that a pharmacist completed follow-up;
- messaging cannot create, change, confirm, cancel, or complete an appointment,
  assessment, follow-up, prescription, or claim; and
- no live provider, patient recipient, or external communication was enabled.

The planning also separated future appointment reminders, pharmacist follow-up
responsibilities, and virtual-care messaging so that one system cannot take over
another system's professional decisions. The Task 07 status is documented in
[`docs/task-07/README.md`](../task-07/README.md).

## Why this work mattered

This work moved the project forward in two practical ways:

1. It gave pharmacists and reviewers a safe, visible example for discussing
   prescription-data extraction without exposing real patient information or
   relying on an unapproved AI service.
2. It established privacy and professional-safety rules for future reminders
   and secure messaging before anyone built a system capable of contacting a
   patient.

Doing the safety and boundary work early reduces the risk of a convenient demo
being mistaken for a clinically approved or production-ready feature.
